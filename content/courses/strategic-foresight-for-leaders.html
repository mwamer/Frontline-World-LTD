Strategic Foresight for Leaders

Develop the ability to look beyond immediate pressures, explore plausible futures, recognise emerging change and strengthen strategic decision-making under uncertainty.

Discuss a Programme Explore the Course ↓

Course Overview

Prepare for change before it becomes disruption.

Strategic foresight helps leaders move from reacting to uncertainty towards systematically exploring what may be changing, what could happen next, and what strategic choices those possibilities create.

The course introduces practical ways of thinking about uncertainty, signals, trends, drivers of change, scenarios and strategic implications.

Foresight is not about predicting the future. It is about developing the capacity to explore alternative possibilities and make better-informed strategic decisions.

Why This Course

From uncertainty to strategic preparedness.

Organisations increasingly operate in environments shaped by rapid technological, political, economic and social change. Foresight provides a structured way to engage with that uncertainty without pretending that the future can be predicted.

01

See Emerging Change

Identify signals, patterns and drivers that can reveal important changes before they become obvious.

02

Think Beyond One Future

Explore multiple plausible futures rather than relying on a single forecast or assumed trajectory.

03

Strengthen Decisions

Translate alternative futures into strategic questions, options and actions that can improve preparedness.

Three Themes

Three connected dimensions of foresight.

The course connects ways of seeing change with ways of interpreting uncertainty and acting strategically.

Theme 01

Reading Change

Signals, trends, drivers, weak signals and the wider forces that may influence future conditions.

Theme 02

Exploring Futures

Plausible futures, uncertainty, assumptions and scenarios as tools for widening strategic imagination.

Theme 03

Acting Strategically

Strategic implications, options, preparedness and ways of embedding foresight into organisational practice.
