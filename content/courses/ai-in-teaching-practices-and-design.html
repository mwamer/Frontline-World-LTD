Overview

Course overview

AI in Teaching: Practices & Design explores how generative AI is changing teaching, learning and educational practice.

The course moves beyond simply learning how to use AI tools. It focuses on how educators can make informed decisions about when, why and how AI should be used in teaching, while protecting learning, academic integrity, critical thinking and human judgement.

Participants explore practical approaches to using AI for lesson and course preparation, learning activities, feedback, assessment and student support. The course also considers how educators can redesign teaching practices in response to the growing presence of AI in education.

The emphasis is on practical experimentation, critical reflection and educational design, rather than technology for its own sake.

The course can also be adapted as a bespoke programme for schools, universities, professional training organisations and education teams.

Content

What the course covers

  • 01

    Understanding AI in Education

    • Generative AI and large language models in accessible terms
    • What AI can and cannot do in educational settings
    • Opportunities, limitations and common misconceptions
    • AI literacy for educators and learners
  • 02

    AI for Teaching Practice

    • Using AI to support lesson and course preparation
    • Generating examples, explanations and learning materials
    • Developing activities, questions and discussion prompts
    • Adapting materials for different learners and contexts
  • 03

    Designing Learning with AI

    • Designing meaningful AI-supported learning activities
    • Prompting as a teaching and learning practice
    • Human–AI collaboration
    • Keeping learning objectives at the centre of AI use
    • Designing activities that develop critical thinking rather than passive AI dependence
  • 04

    Assessment in the Age of AI

    • Rethinking assessment when students have access to generative AI
    • Designing authentic and process-oriented assessment
    • Using AI appropriately for formative feedback
    • Academic integrity and responsible AI use
    • Moving from detection towards better assessment design
  • 05

    Critical and Responsible AI Use

    • Bias, accuracy and hallucination
    • Privacy and data protection
    • Copyright and intellectual property
    • Transparency and disclosure
    • Human judgement, responsibility and professional ethics
  • 06

    From AI Tools to Teaching Strategy

    • Developing an institutional or personal approach to AI
    • Identifying where AI adds genuine educational value
    • Creating practical guidelines for AI use
    • Designing an AI-informed teaching practice
    • Planning next steps for implementation

Approach

Learning approach

The course combines short expert inputs, practical demonstrations, discussion, hands-on experimentation and design activities.

Participants work with real teaching scenarios and develop or redesign an educational activity using AI. The course is designed to be relevant across disciplines rather than tied to a particular subject.

Audience

Who it is for

  • School teachers and educators
  • University lecturers and academic staff
  • Researchers involved in teaching and supervision
  • Professional trainers and learning designers
  • Educational leaders and programme managers
  • Academic and professional development teams
  • Educators beginning to explore AI, or seeking to develop their existing practice
