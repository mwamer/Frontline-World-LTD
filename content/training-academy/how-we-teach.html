How We Teach

From the firm’s own work to professional practice.

Frontline World programmes begin as work the firm has done — strategic research, foresight, communication, and digital practice — and the Academy structures that work into learning. The aim is a method people can apply to decisions that are actually theirs, not information they have received.

{{< learning-arc >}}

One line, four states: material gathered, drawn through a single method, tested against the participant’s own decisions, and settled into practice.

The model

Six movements, one continuous line

Not six modules but six movements along one line. Learning is treated as a single argument with stages rather than a list of separable topics, and each movement is placed on the spine with its purpose in the margin.

  1. 01

    Research

    Programmes begin in the firm’s own work — strategic research, foresight, communication, and digital practice. The starting point is work the firm has already done.

    Where the material comes from

  2. 02

    Evidence

    Findings arrive with the basis that produced them. What the evidence supports, and where its limits begin, is part of the material rather than something left out of it.

    How the material is tested

  3. 03

    Analysis

    Methods are handed over as frameworks — something to work with while a decision is still open, rather than a definition. The aim is a way of working, not a summary of one.

    The shape of the method

  4. 04

    Practice

    Courses run in small cohorts, online and in person, so discussion and practical application sit together. Participants work on decisions that are genuinely theirs rather than on case studies handed down from outside.

    Conditions of learning

  5. 05

    Application

    The method is then run against the participant’s own institutional context — their priorities, their constraints, their colleagues — so what is learned is usable there rather than in general.

    Where the method is used

  6. 06

    Strategic reflection

    Reflection closes the sequence. The point is not familiarity with a topic but whether a participant can hold a position, explain the reasoning behind it, and act on it once the course has ended.

    What is taken away

Principles

Six principles, stated plainly

These are the commitments the Academy’s programmes and courses are built around. They describe how learning is designed, not what a participant will go on to achieve.

  1. 01

    Research-informed

    Programme material comes from the firm’s own research, foresight, communication, and digital practice.

  2. 02

    Frameworks, not summaries

    What is taught is a way of working that survives contact with a decision, not a description of one.

  3. 03

    Applied to real decisions

    Work is done on the decisions participants actually face, not on case studies handed down from above.

  4. 04

    Small cohorts

    Courses run in small cohorts, online and in person, so discussion and practical application sit together rather than one standing in for the other.

  5. 05

    Judgement over claims

    Participants are expected to establish what a claim or a method actually supports, including its limits, rather than accept it or dismiss it.

  6. 06

    Capability that transfers

    The purpose is capability that works in the organisation after the course, not familiarity with a topic.

In practice

How people encounter it

Courses run in small cohorts, online and in person, and can be delivered in-house for organisations. Where a programme needs to sit inside a particular institutional context, the structure is developed around it.

  • Interactive learning

    Combining discussion with practical application.

  • Online

    Delivered remotely.

  • In person

    Delivered face to face.

  • In-house

    Delivered inside an organisation.
